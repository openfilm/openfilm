The assets drawer has a library: its categories along the top (Media, Voices, Sounds, Music, Text, Stickers, Filters, Effects, Transitions, Templates) and each one's groups down the left, OpenFilm's own transitions, effects, filters, text, stickers and sounds free to use in any film. Point at a thing and it plays; click it to try it on the picture in the viewer; double-click it, press Enter or click its + to put it in at the playhead (a transition on the cut, a filter on the picked clips, else the clip under the playhead), then change it in the inspector. Drag a filter onto a clip, or a dissolve onto a cut. Star what you use often: it is in Favorites in every project.

Voices lists the ready-made voices of ElevenLabs (Eleven v4), Inworld (TTS-2) and Cartesia (Sonic 3.6), as each service lists them, by service, language and gender. Click one to hear its sample; double-click it, press Enter or click its + to put it in the chat, and your agent makes the voice-over in it. A voice whose service is not connected yet says where to connect it.

Media in the library is what came into the project, folded under Imported: its own media, and what it took from the library; then Saved. Sounds show their waveform across the row, lit as they play. One highlight color, the timeline's teal, in every pane.

Sounds and Music hold about 5,400 sound effects and 1,200 pieces of music, each with its own name and group in five languages, every piece of music with a photo cover of its mood.

Text starts from one line of words whose look and motion are its own to change: basic lines (title, subtitle, body, note, a line below), 36 word-art looks and motions in, while shown and out. Click one to see it; double-click it to put a new line in with it, or onto the words picked. A line's way out is timed by its clip, however long it is trimmed. And 58 text templates, each at its own size: titles, name cards, callouts, shop prices and buttons, explainers, chapters, endings, variety-show words, chat bubbles, news bars, places and dates, holiday greetings; their words and colors are properties in the inspector.

Stickers hold 1,587 emoji in 3D, Microsoft's Fluent Emoji, named in five languages and grouped (faces, people, animals and nature, food, activities, travel, objects, symbols); each comes in with a pop, at its own size in the middle of the picture. And 45 stickers that move: hand-drawn arrows and marks, emphasis, follow, like and subscribe buttons, badges and decorations.

Filters are 66 color grades in ten groups (film, cinema, portrait, food, scenery, city, fresh, vintage, black and white, mood), each shown on a photo of its kind. A filter mixes the channels and bends a tone curve per channel, so it can tint the shadows one way and the highlights another, as a grade does; the inspector shows the filter a clip wears with its strength, and the adjustments go on after it.

Effects hold 70 overlays: grain, light leaks and flares, snow, petals and gold dust, fireflies and bokeh, 8 mm film and a DV camcorder, frames from a phone to a gold frame, variety-show speed lines and marks, dreamy sparkles, confetti, fireworks and balloons. Transitions hold 58 that cover their cut: flashes and light, shapes, motion graphics, glitches, film, comic, smoke and ink, hearts and confetti; besides the timeline's dissolves.

Templates are short films to make your own: places numbered for your videos and photos (replace each, or drop one on it), with titles, stickers, effects, transitions and music already on them. They lay out for wide and tall films alike.
