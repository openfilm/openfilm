The canvas's toolbar adds a node with one click: text, a picture, a sound, a clip or a cut, each in view and clear of the others, beside the libraries and upload. A small bar at the bottom left, matching the one at the bottom right, holds the keys (their panel opens in the middle, over the toolbar) and one button, always lit, that switches between select and the hand; a switched-on button in either bar now has a clearly lighter ground. A double click on the board opens the add menu every time (the press that closes a menu is not half of one), and the canvas's menus (add, right-click, libraries, zoom, text style, alignment, colors) share one size and look. An empty canvas says "Double-click the canvas to add a node", the toolbar's buttons sink and spring back when pressed, a new node comes in with a small pop, and a double click on the board leaves a ring where it was. The Beta tag is gone.

A text node is a document now: empty it says "Double-click to write"; written in, a bar over it sets a heading or body
text, a bulleted or numbered list, a quote, the alignment, bold, struck, italic and underlined words, undo and redo,
and opens the note large in a window of its own, with its name as a Markdown file to download. Selected, its bar sets
its background color, opens it large or downloads it. Its words stay Markdown (underline and alignment as the HTML
Markdown allows), and its color is kept in the canvas file. Its panel's "+" adds references to write from, and the
four quick starts are gone. A text node is named "Text 1", not "Text node 1", and a double click on its name renames it, on the board or in its window, whose top is a title bar clear of the window's buttons that drags the window. Selected, its words scroll; they show no spelling marks.
