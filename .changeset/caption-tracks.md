Caption tracks: a track in film.html can hold one `<track kind="captions" src="captions/….vtt">`, a standard WebVTT file on the film's time, and the film draws its cues over the tracks below it. The look is the CSS of the file's STYLE blocks: `::cue` for the whole track, `::cue(#c3)` for one cue, `<c.em>` spans, voices, and word timestamps with `:past` / `:future`. Any CSS property works there, animations included. Cue settings and REGION place the cues. A cue's words also take the library text's CSS variables (`--font`, `--size`, `--fill`, `--stroke`, `--glow`, `--box`, `--in`, `--loop`, `--out`…), its fonts and motions included, as a text does. Preview, `look` and exports all draw them, and SRT/VTT files are written from them.

In Studio, on the timeline, a caption track is a narrow text row, each cue a block with a caption mark before its words, and a clip like any other: it is selected, boxed with other clips, dragged (to another caption track too), trimmed, split with ⌘B, copied, cut and pasted (its look with it), deleted, rippled and carried by linkage by the same gestures and edits, and one undo takes back what an edit did to clips and captions together. Nothing but captions goes onto a caption track, and captions go onto nothing else. A caption is added from the row's menu, its words typed in the inspector (not past the film's end). The inspector changes the look of all captions, or with "Apply to all captions" unticked only the selected ones: size, weight, color, outline, background and height on the picture. It says when a selected cue has a look of its own, and puts it back to the track's. Every edit is one undo step, and a file the agent changes shows at once.
