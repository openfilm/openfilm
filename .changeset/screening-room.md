A new workbench, Screening, for people who have never edited: the film plays large, as it will be exported, and is watched as on any video player: under it a progress bar parted at the film's cuts (a click goes there, a drag runs the picture along) and the player's row of controls — play, volume, the time; zoom, settings (speed), how Screening is used, full screen. Pointing at what to change is as plain: above the progress bar, a click points at that moment and a drag at a stretch, the picture following; a drag on the picture circles a part of it. Each goes into the chat, numbered there and on the bar: a number goes back to it, its × takes it out. Scrolling or pinching over the bar, or the zoom control, brings the film closer, down to a second across it, for exact moments and stretches; a line under the bar then shows the part shown, to drag along or widen. The help button (or ⌘ held, Ctrl on Windows) shows how Screening is used, step by step, and its keys. When the agent changes the film, Screening says how many places changed and plays them one after another. It comes with a skill that has agents treat what is pointed at as a director's notes: change only that, never render a video just to show it, and link each change.

The top bar's workbench menu lists each workbench by name with its key: ⌥1, ⌥2… (Alt on Windows) show them in order, and ⌘E (Ctrl+E) goes back to the one shown before.
