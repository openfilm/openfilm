A new workbench, Screening, for people who have never edited: the film plays large, as it will be exported, with one bar under it — play, the time, the whole film as a strip of its frames, the speed — and pointing at what to change is one gesture: Point (or M) for this moment, a drag on the picture to circle a part, a drag on the strip to pick a stretch; each of the last two says so where it is done until it has been used once. Each goes into the chat, numbered there and on the film: a number goes back to it, its × takes it out. Hovering the strip shows the frame there. Scrolling or pinching over the strip brings the film closer, down to a second across it, for exact moments and stretches; a line under the bar then shows the whole film and the part shown, to drag along, and the playhead stays in sight as it plays. When the agent changes the film, Screening says how many places changed and plays them one after another. Holding ⌘ (Ctrl on Windows) shows its shortcuts. It comes with a skill that has agents treat what is pointed at as a director's notes: change only that, never render a video just to show it, and link each change.

The top bar's workbench menu lists each workbench by name with its key: ⌥1, ⌥2… (Alt on Windows) show them in order, and ⌘E (Ctrl+E) goes back to the one shown before.
