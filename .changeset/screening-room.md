A new workbench, Screening, for people who have never edited: the film plays large, as it will be exported, with one bar under it — play, the time, the whole film as a strip of its frames, the speed, and a button that shows how it is used (holding ⌘, or Ctrl on Windows, shows the same). Pointing at what to change is what anyone does with a player: a double click on the bar (or M) for that moment, a drag on the bar for a stretch, the picture following the pointer so the stretch is picked by what is in it, and a drag on the picture to circle a part. Each says so where it is done until it has been used once. Each goes into the chat, numbered there and on the film: a number goes back to it, its × takes it out. Scrolling or pinching over the bar brings the film closer, down to a second across it, for exact moments and stretches; a scrollbar along its foot then shows the part shown and drags it along, and the playhead stays in sight as it plays. When the agent changes the film, Screening says how many places changed and plays them one after another. It comes with a skill that has agents treat what is pointed at as a director's notes: change only that, never render a video just to show it, and link each change.

The top bar's workbench menu lists each workbench by name with its key: ⌥1, ⌥2… (Alt on Windows) show them in order, and ⌘E (Ctrl+E) goes back to the one shown before.
