A new workbench, Screening, for people who have never edited: the film plays large, as it will be exported, and is watched and pointed at as with any player. Under it, one bar: play, the time, the whole film as a strip of its frames (a click goes there, a drag runs the picture along), the speed, and a button that shows how Screening is used, step by step, with its keys (holding ⌘, or Ctrl on Windows, shows the same). Above the strip, a row for pointing: a click there points at that moment, a drag at a stretch, the picture following. A drag on the picture circles a part of it. Each goes into the chat, numbered there and on the bar: a number goes back to it, its × takes it out. Below the strip, a zoom bar shows the part of the film shown: its ends bring the film closer, down to a second across the strip, its middle moves along it; scrolling or pinching over the bar does the same. When the agent changes the film, Screening says how many places changed and plays them one after another. It comes with a skill that has agents treat what is pointed at as a director's notes: change only that, never render a video just to show it, and link each change.

The top bar's workbench menu lists each workbench by name with its key: ⌥1, ⌥2… (Alt on Windows) show them in order, and ⌘E (Ctrl+E) goes back to the one shown before.
