Exports of footage several times faster. A film's frames were each drawn in a browser and taken as a picture, footage
too. Now a stretch that is nothing but footage is read straight from its file (on the graphics chip where it can be)
and given to the same encoder: a clip that fills the stage, or stands in it with bars, with no effect of its own, and
any captions over it, each line drawn once and laid over. Titles, effects, pages and anything else are drawn as before,
and the two join frame for frame. Measured on a two-minute cut of 1080p footage with captions: 25 seconds, where it
took 77.

The picture is better too: the footage is encoded once, from the file, without the copy and the JPEG that stood
between it and the film. Such an export is as sharp as its footage, so at the same quality its file is larger than
before; Standard now gives the picture High gave, in a smaller file.

Before any footage is read this way, a frame of each file is drawn both ways and has to be the same picture, so what
you export is still what the preview shows. Footage a browser shows in its own way (HDR, ten bits) is drawn as before.
