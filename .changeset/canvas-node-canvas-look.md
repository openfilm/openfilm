The canvas looks and works as the node canvases people know: a near-black board with a fine dot grid; cards a step lighter with a grey line (a light one when selected), named above in grey and numbered as they were added ("Picture node 2"); a picture's or a clip's card is its shape at 350 high, and takes a new shape when another is asked. An empty card shows its kind large and what to start with: a new text offers writing in it, or a picture, a clip or music made from it; a clip offers being made from a first frame, or between a first and a last. An empty canvas says to double-click it and offers a picture, a clip, audio, a script (asked of the agent) or a cut.

The panel under a selected node is wider and rounder, with a button to write in a taller box. A picture node has presets — storyboards, the next moments of a picture, natural skin, cinematic light, a panorama, nine cameras, and character, face, location and product sheets — each a tag before its words that names the node and sets its shape and quality. A clip is made in one of five ways its model makes: from words, everything as reference, from a picture, between first and last frames, or from picture references. "From the canvas" picks what goes in by clicking it on the board. Model lists show each service's mark.

A selected picture can be seen from nine angles, split into its 2×2 or 3×3 cells, or have its background taken out; a clip gives the frame it shows. A group shows a bar with its color, Make all, keep as a template and ungroup, and its name small above it. Text is written with a bar for headings, bold, italic and a divider. The toolbar at the foot has add, move, templates and the keys (in four columns); the bar at the bottom left has the chat, the list of canvas elements, the overview map, wires shown or hidden, snapping to the grid and the zoom. The selected node's wires are blue and flow. The canvas's right-click menus add uploading, undo, redo and copying a picture to the clipboard; Tab adds a node, ⌘0 fits the canvas and ⌘F finds a node.
