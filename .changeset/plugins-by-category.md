What OpenFilm can do has a window of its own, Features, instead of a tab of the library: the blocks beside the chat's "+", or Features in the OpenFilm menu, opens it. Everything in it is one of three kinds, told apart by where it shows when it is on: Workbenches, the places you work in by hand and switch to at the top right; Skills, the know-how your agent uses in the conversation; and Connectors, the outside services and tools your agent reaches (the MCP servers you added to an agent, each let into OpenFilm with its switch). Agents are not a kind: what an agent brings is listed under its kind, with its name. The side has Mine (those three) and Discover: Featured first, a big pick and a shelf per kind, then all of each kind and the community's, as cards with a picture, what it is for, its kind and its button (Install, Turn on, or said Installed or On; what is still being made shows Coming soon). Import, at the side's foot, takes a folder or a link and works out whether it is a workbench or a skill. An item's page sums it up at the top (how hard it is to start with, how little work a result takes, what it costs, where you use it, who it is for), says what it is for and where to open it, and folds what it is made of under For developers. A plugin or a skill says these in its openfilm.json (`category`, `difficulty`, `ease`, `audience`, `cost`, `details`; plugins/README.md). Screening, Motion and the stage have marks of their own.

The large windows (Settings, Features, projects, export, version history) share one size and one look: the side on the left in the panels' color, the page on the right a step lighter in the dark (a step darker side in the light), and Esc pressed in one is its own.
