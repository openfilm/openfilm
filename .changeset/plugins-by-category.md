What OpenFilm can do has a window of its own, Features, instead of a tab of the library: the grid beside the chat's "+", or Features in the OpenFilm menu, opens it. Everything in it is one of two kinds, told apart by where it shows when it is on: Workbenches, the places you work in by hand and switch to at the top right (canvas, stage, screening, edit, Motion, color), and Skills, the know-how your agent uses in the conversation. Each list says what it is in one line, and each item is a row with its mark, its name, what it is for and its switch. Get more lists what there is to install and keeps what you install, marked Installed; Import a skill sits at the side's foot. An item's page sums it up at the top (how hard it is to start with, how little work a result takes, what it costs, where you use it, who it is for), says what it is for and where to open it, and folds what it is made of under For developers. A plugin or a skill says these in its openfilm.json (`category`, `difficulty`, `ease`, `audience`, `cost`, `details`; plugins/README.md). Screening, Motion and the stage have marks of their own.

The large windows (Settings, Features, projects, export, version history) share one size and one look: the side on the left in the panels' color, the page on the right a step lighter in the dark (a step darker side in the light), and Esc pressed in one is its own.
